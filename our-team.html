Tera Osborne

Executive Director

Read more about Tera Osborne

Tera is a Registered Nurse and loves working with Indigenous communities to help improve overall health and wellness. Our goal as a team is to expand our services to cover our catchment area and provide services tailored to the needs of each community we care for.

Tera was born and raised in Napanee and continues to live there now. She has a husband, Justin, and 3 children, Isaac, Jack, and Mia. Tera’s maternal grandmother was Indigenous. Her father was Algonquin and grew up in the Pembroke area; her mother was Metis and grew up near Barry’s Bay. Unfortunately, her grandmother was not exposed to her parents' culture growing up, so Tera continues to learn about the important history of Indigenous People and how important it is to incorporate culture into overall wellness.

Dr. Jamaica Cass, MD, PhD, CCFP DABOM

Physician

Read more about Dr. Cass

Dr. Jamaica Cass, a member of the Mohawks of the Bay of Quinte, provides both primary care and metabolic health care at the IIPCT. She grew up nearby in Belleville, and received her BScH in biological science from the University of Guelph, her PhD in breast cancer molecular biology from Queen’s University, and her MD from Tulane University in New Orleans, Louisiana. Prior to family medicine training, Dr. Cass spent three years training as a general surgeon. Outside of clinical practice, her professional interests are all driven by her desire to advance reconciliation in medical education and healthcare. She is the first Director of the Queen’s Weeneebayko Health Education Program and the Director of Indigenous Health at Queen’s University.

Dr. Cass is the first Indigenous woman in Canada to earn both an MD and a PhD. A national leader in Indigenous health education, she has driven advocacy and change through leadership roles with Ontario Health, the Indigenous Physicians Association of Canada, the Medical Council of Canada, the National Circle on Indigenous Medical Education, and the Indigenous Health Committee of the Association of Faculties of Medicine of Canada. She holds faculty appointments at both Queen’s University and Toronto Metropolitan University. Her work in education, mentorship, and community impact has earned numerous honours, including the King Charles III Coronation Medal.

Outside of professional interests, Dr. Cass enjoys creating beadwork and garments blending Indigenous and contemporary elements.

Andrew Alkenbrack

Registered Psychotherapist

Read more about Andrew Alkenbrack

Originally from the Tyendinaga area, Andrew has worked in the mental health field for over 25 years, in both private practice and in a hospital setting. Clinical experience and training with children, adolescents, and adults over the years allow for treating a diverse population.

Andrew has an undergraduate degree in Psychology from York University and a Master of Arts in Counselling from Yorkville University. He is a Registered Psychotherapist with the College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario (# 001878).

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) is his usual modality of therapy, which is often identified as one of the most effective forms of therapy for dealing with mood and anxiety problems. As a member of the Mohawks of the Bay of Quinte, Andrew also utilizes a First Nations perspective on mental and overall health, with a focus on balance in various aspects of one’s life. Andrew also received training in Solution-Focused Brief Therapy, EMDR, and Anger Solutions.

His approach is client-centred, with a focus on helping clients identify and achieve their goals. Andrew looks forward to helping you navigate life’s journey.
